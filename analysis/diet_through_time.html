ARID — Isotopic variation through time

δ¹³C · δ¹⁵N (organic tissue) · δ¹⁸O (carbonate tissue) across regions and ecozones of northern Chile

What this shows: isotopic values summarized as median ± IQR for groups with ≥ 3 individuals, plotted against time (BCE/CE). Bubble size is proportional to sample size.
  • δ¹³C reflects dietary carbon: more negative = C3-dominated diet (plants, marine); less negative = C4 inputs (maize, chenopods) or high marine protein.
  • δ¹⁵N reflects trophic level, aridity, and marine protein intake. High values (>20‰) are typical of coastal diets in the Atacama.
  • δ¹⁸O (bone apatite) reflects water sources and mobility. More negative = higher altitude or cooler/wetter sources.

Periods with mixed assignments (e.g. "Formative/Middle") are excluded. Only groups with n ≥ 3 are shown.

Patterns in δ¹³C

  • Coast (all regions): relatively enriched δ¹³C (–14 to –10‰) throughout, consistent with sustained high marine protein intake. Arica y Parinacota Coast shows a marked enrichment in the Late period (median ~–10.9‰), possibly linked to intensified coastal exploitation or maize consumption.
  • Lowlands (Arica y Parinacota): gradual enrichment from Formative (–15.4‰) to Late Intermediate (–11.9‰), consistent with increasing maize and C4-plant contribution to the diet over time.
  • Lowlands (Tarapacá): sharp enrichment between Formative (–15.7‰) and Late Intermediate (–10.0‰) — one of the steepest temporal shifts in the dataset, suggesting rapid dietary change.
  • Precordillera (Antofagasta): consistently depleted δ¹³C (–17 to –14‰), reflecting C3-dominated agriculture (tubers, quinoa) throughout the Middle and Late Intermediate periods.

Patterns in δ¹⁵N

  • Coast: persistently high δ¹⁵N (22–27‰) across all periods and regions, a hallmark of marine protein consumption amplified by hyper-arid Atacama baseline enrichment.
  • Arica y Parinacota Lowlands: δ¹⁵N increases from Formative (16.4‰) to Late Intermediate (20.5‰), suggesting greater reliance on animal protein or enriched water sources in later periods.
  • Tarapacá Lowlands: low δ¹⁵N in the Formative (12.5‰) vs. elevated Late Intermediate (16.5‰), possibly reflecting a dietary shift toward animal products or increased aridity effects.
  • Precordillera (Antofagasta): strikingly low and stable δ¹⁵N (~11‰) across Formative, Middle, and Late Intermediate, consistent with C3 plant-based diets and freshwater sources at altitude.

Patterns in δ¹⁸O

  • Antofagasta Coast: δ¹⁸O increases from Archaic (–2.95‰) to Formative (–1.4‰), suggesting a shift toward more evaporated or lower-altitude water sources in later periods.
  • Antofagasta Precordillera: consistently depleted values (~–5‰) in Formative and Middle periods, consistent with high-altitude meteoric water (Andean precipitation). Slight enrichment in Late Intermediate may reflect increased mobility or use of different water sources.
  • Antofagasta Lowlands: more depleted values in Late Intermediate (–5.2‰) than Formative (–2.95‰), possibly indicating greater use of Andean-sourced river water in later periods.
  • Tarapacá Lowlands: very depleted δ¹⁸O in Late Intermediate (–8.2‰), the most negative in the dataset, suggesting strong reliance on Andean-origin water (rivers flowing from the altiplano).
  • Caution: δ¹⁸O coverage is uneven — most data comes from Antofagasta (San Pedro de Atacama area). Patterns from regions with few groups (n < 3 excluded) should be interpreted carefully.

Reading the biplot

  • Upper-right quadrant (δ¹³C > –13, δ¹⁵N > 20): coastal diet dominated by marine protein, characteristic of all coastal groups regardless of period.
  • Lower-left quadrant (δ¹³C < –15, δ¹⁵N < 14): C3 plant-based diet with low trophic level — Precordillera and some inland Formative groups (Antofagasta, Tarapacá).
  • Temporal trajectory (Lowlands): Lowland groups shift from lower-left (Formative) toward upper-right (Late Intermediate), indicating a convergence toward more enriched diets combining C4 plants and/or animal protein over time.
  • Groups in the upper-left (enriched δ¹⁵N, depleted δ¹³C) may reflect diets high in freshwater fish or herbivores feeding on C3 plants.
